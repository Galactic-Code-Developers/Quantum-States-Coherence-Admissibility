## Appendix B — Formal Dependency Graph and Research Sequence

### Purpose

This appendix defines a formal dependency graph organizing the present work and
a sequence of planned extensions. The labels *Stage I* through *Stage VI* are
internal roadmap indices used solely to track logical and conceptual dependencies.
They are not paper titles, do not correspond to journal submission order, and do not
imply that later stages are required for the validity of earlier ones.

The purpose of this dependency graph is to ensure conceptual hygiene: results
established at an earlier stage remain valid even if later stages fail, and no
extension retroactively alters the assumptions or conclusions of prior stages.

---

### Stage I — *Quantum States and Coherence-Admissibility* (Foundational Result)

**Role in the sequence.**  
Stage I establishes the foundational criterion of coherence-admissibility as a
necessary condition for quantum-state determinacy. It answers the question of
*when* a quantum-state description is physically valid, without assuming collapse,
observer dependence, or universal state assignment.

**Dependencies.**  
Stage I depends only on standard quantum mechanics, open-system dynamics, and
operational notions of coherence and decoherence. It does not depend on any
microscopic vacuum model, relativistic formulation, or cosmological dynamics.

**Downstream use.**  
All later stages treat coherence-admissibility as an input condition; none modify
or reinterpret the Stage I criterion.

---

### Stage II — Microscopic Origin of Admissibility Thresholds

**Role in the sequence.**  
Stage II investigates how admissibility thresholds ε may arise from concrete
system–environment interactions, noise spectra, and measurement coarse-graining.

**Dependencies.**  
Stage II depends on Stage I but introduces no new assumptions into it.

**Downstream use.**  
Results from Stage II may inform experimental modeling but do not alter the logical
structure of admissibility.

---

### Stage III — Structural Basis Selection and Admissible Representations

**Role in the sequence.**  
Stage III addresses how interaction-relevant or dynamically preferred
representations emerge within coherence-admissible domains without postulating a
universal preferred basis.

**Dependencies.**  
Stage III depends on Stages I and II.

**Downstream use.**  
Stage III provides structural interpretation but does not modify admissibility.

---

### Stage IV — Vacuum-Coherence Substrates and State Stability

**Role in the sequence.**  
Stage IV explores whether vacuum or substrate structure modulates coherence
lifetimes or admissibility boundaries without modifying dynamical field equations.

**Dependencies.**  
Stage IV depends on Stages I–III and is explicitly optional.

**Downstream use.**  
Vacuum effects are treated as modulatory rather than foundational.

---

### Stage V — Relativistic Formulation of Coherence-Admissibility

**Role in the sequence.**  
Stage V seeks a relativistically covariant formulation of coherence-admissibility.

**Dependencies.**  
Stage V depends on Stages I–IV.

**Downstream use.**  
Failure restricts admissibility to nonrelativistic or foliation-fixed settings.

---

### Stage VI — Large-Scale Coherence and Emergent Phenomenology

**Role in the sequence.**  
Stage VI investigates whether large-scale coherence structure interfaces with
gravitational or cosmological phenomenology through state-validity conditions.

**Dependencies.**  
Stage VI depends on Stages I–V.

**Downstream use.**  
Failure constrains phenomenological relevance only.

---

### Summary of Dependency Structure

Stage I → Stage II → Stage III → Stage IV → Stage V → Stage VI

Each stage is independently falsifiable. Failure beyond Stage I does not
invalidate the foundational admissibility criterion.

## Appendix C — Formal Falsification Map Aligned with the Dependency Graph

This appendix provides a falsification map aligned with the dependency structure
above. Failure at a given stage identifies precisely which layer is invalidated
without retroactively contaminating earlier results.

---

### Stage I — Foundational Criterion

**Target claim.**  
Quantum-state determinacy is physically valid only within coherence-admissible
domains.

**Falsification condition.**  
A reproducible regime exists where coherence diagnostics fall below calibrated
thresholds, structural stability fails, and off-diagonal suppression is confirmed,
yet a globally predictive quantum-state description remains operationally valid.

**Implication.**  
The coherence-admissibility criterion is false.

---

### Stage II — Threshold Origin

**Falsification condition.**  
No mechanistic mapping from environment and noise to admissibility thresholds can
be established across platforms.

**Implication.**  
Stage II fails; Stage I remains operationally valid.

---

### Stage III — Basis Selection

**Falsification condition.**  
Admissibility holds but no interaction-relevant representation is dynamically
selected.

**Implication.**  
Basis-selection extension fails.

---

### Stage IV — Vacuum Modulation

**Falsification condition.**  
Vacuum/substrate proxies show no correlation with admissibility boundaries when
predicted.

**Implication.**  
Vacuum-structured extensions fail.

---

### Stage V — Covariance

**Falsification condition.**  
Covariant formulations yield observer-dependent admissibility assignments with
experimentally distinguishable consequences.

**Implication.**  
Relativistic extension fails.

---

### Stage VI — Phenomenology

**Falsification condition.**  
Admissibility-linked coherence variations cannot produce measurable effects or are
excluded by data.

**Implication.**  
Phenomenological interface fails.

---

### Summary

Failure at later stages never retroactively invalidates Stage I unless a direct
counterexample to the necessity of coherence-admissibility is obtained.
